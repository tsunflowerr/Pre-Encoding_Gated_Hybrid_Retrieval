# Introduction

Sparse and dense retrieval have different strengths at the query level. A lexical matcher can recover exact technical terms without a neural query encoder, while a dense retriever can help when wording differs from relevant documents. Running both for every query pays the dense cost even when the sparse ranking is already adequate. Prior work has studied selecting sparse, dense, or hybrid strategies from query features under an efficiency budget [Arabzadeh et al., 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10739). The [BEIR benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08663) also motivates evaluating retrieval choices across different domains and against a strong BM25 baseline.

We study **pre-encoding gated hybrid retrieval (PE-GH)** on the Programmers and Unix domains of CQADupStack. PE-GH first retrieves BM25 top-100 and computes inexpensive lexical, query-text, and BM25-ranking features. A fold-specific Ridge model estimates the change in nDCG@10 from replacing BM25 with a fixed sparse–dense fusion. Only when its score exceeds a validation-selected threshold does the system encode the query, search the dense index, and fuse the two rankings. Queries with no sparse vocabulary match take the dense branch; empty queries return an empty ranking. The gate never uses a query embedding, test relevance label, or quality cache at online inference.

The central question is whether PE-GH can retain the quality of an always-hybrid policy while making fewer dense calls. We evaluate five group-separated folds in each domain. Fusion settings, training labels, models, and thresholds are chosen using train and validation data; each query appears in one out-of-fold test bucket. The primary comparison is PE-GH versus the validation-selected static baseline S* in macro nDCG@10, with a preregistered noninferiority margin of 0.01 and a group-aware paired bootstrap. The measurement harness records fresh encoder calls and full request latency in separate processes.

On 1,948 out-of-fold test queries, PE-GH makes 1,125 dense calls (57.75%) and obtains macro nDCG@10 of 0.31216, compared with 0.31463 for S*. The observed difference is −0.00246, and the one-sided 95% lower bound is −0.00601, above the −0.01 margin. The result supports the registered **quality** noninferiority criterion for these fixed predictions. In the complete five-repeat online benchmark, all 29,220 ranking-ID sequences match their frozen offline runs. PE-GH's pooled mean latency is 665.3 ms versus 668.2 ms for S*, but it is slower on Programmers and faster on Unix. This small, uneven difference does not support a general latency speedup claim; CPU use and dense calls fall more clearly.

Our contribution is an auditable evaluation of a deliberately small gate: a train-only scaler and Ridge model over 21 pre-dense features, validation-only operating points, strict artifact binding, and direct encoder-call accounting. We report negative fold outcomes, static and learned controls, and post-freeze random and oracle diagnostics alongside the primary result. The scope is two technical-question domains and one pinned dense model; cross-domain or hardware-general latency claims require further experiments.
